DARKSOL / THE ESSENTIALS

Introduction

DarkSol helps you send and receive privately by comparing privacy pools and routing transfers through supported protocols.

Choose a route using real pool activity, estimated privacy, and cost. Your wallet approves the transactions; the selected protocol handles the shielded funds.

Private send

Send from your wallet through a privacy pool to a recipient. Choose the asset, amount, and destination; DarkSol compares the available routes for you, then you approve the transfer. The pool helps separate the deposit from the withdrawal. See how private transfers work.

Private receive

Private transfers

A private transfer moves funds through a privacy pool so the recipient's payment isn't directly linked to your wallet. You deposit into the pool, and the funds leave it separately to your recipient. DarkSol picks the pool for you from live pool activity; your wallet approves every transaction that moves your funds.

  1. EnterOpen Private transfer in the app, choose a token (SOL, USDC or USDT) and an amount, and add one or more recipients. With several recipients, their amounts must add up to the total.
  2. CompareAs soon as the form is complete, DarkSol compares the supported pools and picks the route most likely to reach good privacy soonest, using fees to break close ties. Open Details to see the estimated privacy, pool size, similar-size activity, expected wait and estimated fee.
  3. DepositYour wallet approves one deposit into the selected pool. Privacy Cash also asks you to sign a sign-in message once per browser; it moves no funds. Umbra v2 asks you to sign an unlock message once per tab, and both Umbra versions ask for a one-time registration.
  4. DeliverThe funds leave the pool to your recipient. On Privacy Cash, Cloak and Umbra v2, the pool's relayer submits this withdrawal and pays its network fee, so there is nothing more to approve. When it lands, the app shows Transfer complete with a link to the transaction.

How routes are scored

The privacy estimate is a score from 0 to 100, based on how deep the pool is compared with your amount, how much recent activity there is, how many flows of a similar size it has seen, how spread out that activity is over time, and how unusual your amount is. A transfer larger than the pool, or a pool with too small a published anonymity set, is marked Not private and can't be sent. The score is an estimate, not a guarantee.

What DarkSol sees

To compare routes, your browser sends only the token, the amount and how it is split between recipients. Your wallet address and your recipients' addresses are not part of route planning. To measure how private each way of sending is, it also sends the token and the exact amount; DarkSol does not log or store that amount. Deposits and withdrawals are public Solana transactions, as with any pool.

More options

Swap
Pick a different token under Receive and the recipient gets that token instead. Privacy Cash and Umbra v2 swap between SOL, USDC and USDT: the pool withdraws your funds privately to a one-time wallet that swaps them through Jupiter and pays the recipient. Cloak swaps SOL to USDC or USDT through its own relay. From USDC or USDT, Jupiter pays that swap's network fee, so it needs at least 4–5 of the token. Keep part shielded and Use shielded funds work for swaps too.
Bridge
Choose Bridge under Receive to deliver to Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Polygon or Bitcoin through Privacy Cash. The amount must be worth at least about 0.05 SOL.
Delay
The clock button delivers within 10, 20 or 30 minutes instead of right away, which puts more pool activity between deposit and delivery. For a delayed delivery, DarkSol's server holds the prepared withdrawal, including its recipient, until it is sent. The recipient and amount are fixed inside it, so it can only be delivered or dropped, never redirected. If it isn't delivered, the funds stay in the pool and Send now sends them. One recipient, Privacy Cash only.
Shielding
The shield button offers Keep part shielded to send some and keep the rest in the pool, Shield only to deposit without sending, and Use shielded funds to pay from what you already hold in a pool, with no new deposit. Every pool supports all three; Cloak keeps what stays shielded in this browser only.

If a transfer is interrupted

Once the deposit lands, your funds are safe in the pool even if the page closes. Privacy Cash and Umbra v2 funds are tied to your wallet: sign in on any device and send them with Use shielded funds. Cloak saves its private notes in this browser only, so send them with Use shielded funds here and don't clear site data until you have.

Payment links

A payment link is an invoice whose funds land in a privacy pool instead of your wallet. Your browser prepares the private note the payment will fund; DarkSol only stores the invoice and tracks whether it was paid. The payer's wallet deposits straight into the pool, and only your wallet can spend what arrives.

  1. CreateChoose a pool, token and amount, plus an optional reference. Your wallet signs two messages, which are used to create your private merchant ID and the private note your payment is sent to. The signatures stay in your browser and never move funds. Your browser builds the note and sends DarkSol only the public invoice: amount, pool, token and the note's commitment. For Privacy Cash, it also sends the note encrypted under your key.
  2. ShareEach invoice gets its own link, darksol.app/pay/<id>. It does not contain or reveal your wallet address.
  3. PayThe payer connects any Solana wallet and approves one deposit into the pool, funding your note. Right before signing, the checkout reloads the invoice and stops if its terms changed or it was already paid. The transaction goes directly to a Solana RPC, not through DarkSol.
  4. ConfirmThe checkout reports the deposit's public transaction ID. DarkSol checks that the deposit is finalized on-chain and matches the invoice, then marks it paid. If the page closed early, the payer can paste the transaction ID to verify it later.
  5. CollectOpen Collect, enter any destination address and sign the same message again. Your browser proves ownership of each paid note, and the pool's relayer submits the withdrawal and pays its network fee. Two payments in the same pool and token are combined into one withdrawal.

Supported pools and tokens

Payment links work with Privacy Cash and Cloak for SOL, USDC and USDT on Solana mainnet. Cloak requires at least 0.01 SOL or 1 USDC/USDT per payment. The payer pays network fees in SOL; Cloak token deposits also need about 0.006 SOL for a one-time lookup table.

Recovery

Your payment links are tied to your wallet, not to this browser. Signing in with the same wallet on any device finds your links and lets you collect them. The download button in Your payment links saves a backup of every invoice record, so a copy exists outside DarkSol. Deleting a link disables it and removes its record from DarkSol. If the link may already have been paid, choose Download and delete.

Red team

The red team tests privacy from an outside observer's perspective. Using only public Solana data, it takes a withdrawal from a privacy pool and does what a chain analyst would: work out which depositor wallet most likely paid for it, and how many others could have. You can run it yourself on the Trace page in the app.

  1. IndexDarkSol records every deposit, withdrawal and note in the Privacy Cash and Cloak pools for SOL, USDC and USDT.
  2. Replay and verifyEach pool is rebuilt from its first note and checked against the proofs on-chain and the live pool account, so the history a trace relies on matches the chain.
  3. TracePaste a withdrawal's tx hash or Solscan link. The trace lists every note that could have paid for it, then applies the rules below to the wallets that deposited them and sorts those wallets into candidates. Paste a deposit instead to see the withdrawals its wallet is the candidate for. A withdrawal from the last few minutes appears after the next indexing cycle.

Reading a report

Verdict
One strongest candidate when a rule below singles out one depositor; N probable candidates when no rule does, but some wallets have a clue that fits, such as a deposit of the same amount within a minute before; Hidden among N wallets when nothing points to any one of the wallets that could have paid; No single wallet could have paid for it when no depositor had enough left in the pool.
Public clues
Shown first: the deposit of the same amount right before the withdrawal, whether anything else happened in that minute and whether the proof was made right after it, then how many deposits match the amount, how busy the pool was in the minute before, and how common the amount is. Amber marks what makes a withdrawal easier to trace. None of these is proof, and they are never combined into a score.
Candidates
The wallets that could have paid, in three tiers. Strongest: the one wallet a rule names. Probable: wallets with a clue that fits, such as a same-amount deposit, exactly the amount left in the pool, or a deposit right before the proof. When more than six wallets share a clue it no longer narrows anything down, so only the one timing backs stays probable. Possible: the other wallets that had enough in the pool, the most recent first, at most 30.
How this was checked
Collapsed under the report: how the pool history was verified, and what the report cannot tell, such as the hidden amount inside each note, or where money held in the pool first came from. While evidence is still being collected, the figures are bounds, not exact values.

Route scoring for private transfers weighs related signals, such as how common your amount is and how much similar-size activity a pool has.

What a trace cannot tell

Amounts inside notes are hidden, so possible sources can't be filtered by size, and the count is of notes, not people. Notes that were already spent can't be identified, so they stay among the possible sources. Only the last hop is analysed: where money held in the pool first came from is not followed, and the recipient's other activity is not checked yet.

A suspected link is an inference, not proof of ownership. Finding no link does not prove anonymity.

Core concepts

Privacy pool
A protocol that holds shielded funds and verifies private transfers.
Shielding
Moving public funds into a pool. Withdrawing makes the destination and amount public again.
Private note
The secret data used to spend shielded funds. Keep recovery material private.
Privacy estimate
A score based on pool activity and your transfer. Amount, timing, and available cover matter; the score is not a guarantee.

Architecture

DarkSol connects three parts:

  1. Browser & walletChoose routes, prepare private proofs, and approve transactions.
  2. Privacy protocolsHold shielded funds and verify deposits and withdrawals on-chain.
  3. Data & planningIndexers collect public activity. Stored data powers pool comparisons, privacy estimates, and red-team analysis.

The payment-link service stores invoices and payment status. Payment-note spending keys are derived in your browser from your wallet signature. See Payment links for the full flow.
