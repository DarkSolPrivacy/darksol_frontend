Get started

Introduction

Send privately in one step, and see how private it really is before you do.

A private transfer should feel like any other send: pick a token, an amount and a recipient. That is how most people already use privacy pools. They deposit and withdraw straight away, and the result is often easy to trace, because a matching deposit made moments earlier points back to the sender.

DarkSol checks before you send. It tries to trace your transfer the way a chain analyst would, against each pool's real history, and shows how hard that would be. Then it suggests the pool and timing that protect you best for that token and amount.

DarkSol never holds your funds. Your wallet approves every transaction, and the pool you choose holds the shielded funds.

Private transfers

A private transfer moves funds through a privacy pool so the recipient's payment isn't directly linked to your wallet. You deposit into the pool, and the funds leave it separately to your recipient. DarkSol picks the pool for you from live pool activity; your wallet approves every transaction that moves your funds.

  1. EnterOpen Private transfer in the app, choose a token (SOL, USDC or USDT) and an amount, and add one or more recipients. With several recipients, their amounts must add up to the total.
  2. CompareDarkSol compares measured privacy, fees and your settings. Expand a pool to see its available waits and privacy bars. An optional suggestion may deposit more and keep the remainder shielded if that improves the measured privacy.
  3. DepositYour wallet approves one deposit into the selected pool. Privacy Cash also asks you to sign a sign-in message once per browser; it moves no funds. Umbra v2 asks you to sign an unlock message once per tab, and both Umbra versions ask for a one-time registration.
  4. DeliverThe funds leave the pool to your recipient through its withdrawal flow. If you chose a wait, the deposit stays shielded until you return and send the pending payment. When the withdrawal lands, the app shows completion with a link to the transaction.

How routes are compared

The send-page meter shows how often hypothetical sends escape our forensic tracing tools against recorded pool history. DarkSol compares that measurement with fees and your settings to suggest an option. See Privacy meter & simulations for the method, the score and its limits. Other planner estimates use pool activity and available cover; they are separate from this meter.

What DarkSol sees

To compare routes, your browser sends the token, amount and how it is split between recipients. Your wallet address and recipients' addresses are not part of route planning. Privacy simulations receive the token, exact withdrawal budget, maximum wait and optional manually chosen deposit. The simulation service does not log or retain those amounts. Actual deposits and withdrawals are public Solana transactions.

More options

Swap
Pick a different token under Receive and the recipient gets that token instead. Cloak swaps SOL to USDC or USDT; Privacy Cash swaps between SOL and USDC or USDT; Umbra v2, which has no swap of its own, withdraws your SOL privately to a one-time wallet that swaps it to USDC or USDT through Jupiter and pays the recipient. Bridge
Choose Bridge under Receive to deliver to Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Polygon or Bitcoin through Privacy Cash. The amount must be worth at least about 0.05 SOL.
Wait
Choose a measured wait within your maximum: 6, 12 or 24 hours where available. You deposit now, and the payment stays pending in this browser until you return and send it. This is not automatic scheduled delivery. The withdrawal proof is prepared when you send, rather than held from the deposit time. See how waits affect the simulation.
Shielding
The shield button offers Keep part shielded to send some and keep the rest in the pool, Shield only to deposit without sending, and Use shielded funds to pay from what you already hold in a pool, with no new deposit. Every pool supports all three; Cloak keeps what stays shielded in this browser only.

If a transfer is interrupted

Once the deposit lands, your funds are safe in the pool even if the page closes. Privacy Cash and Umbra v2 funds are tied to your wallet: sign in on any device and send them with Use shielded funds. Cloak saves its private notes in this browser only, so send them with Use shielded funds here and don't clear site data until you have.

Privacy meter & simulations

We construct hypothetical sends and try to trace them using our forensic model. The simulation uses real indexed pool history and synthetic deposit and withdrawal records. It moves no funds, broadcasts no transaction, and does not execute private proofs or Solana's transaction simulation.

  1. Record the historyOur production forensic linker processes the pool's public history, recording wallet budgets, unspent deposits and earlier withdrawals.
  2. Construct a sendA synthetic sender deposits and withdraws the proposed amounts at historical times, with the selected wait and fees.
  3. Try to trace itA fast probe applies the linker's rules to the hypothetical withdrawal. A second analyst knows our sending strategy and guesses the sender using amounts and timing. A trial counts as traced if either identifies the synthetic sender.
  4. RepeatThe exact-amount simulator runs 300 trials per scenario over up to 90 days of recorded history. Each scenario specifies the pool, token, amount, strategy, deposit candidate and wait.

What the bars mean

The score is 100 × (1 − traced / completed trials), rounded to a whole number. If 60 of 300 trials are traced, the score is 80 and the meter shows 4 of 5 bars. Four or five bars meet the app's private threshold.

Zero bars are reserved for insufficient mathematical cover: the pool's existing funds cannot account for the withdrawal without the new deposit. For example, depositing an amount and immediately withdrawing the same amount, when it exceeds what the pool already holds of that token, necessarily reveals that the new deposit funded at least part of the withdrawal. The current zero-bar check flags immediate withdrawals larger than the pool's known token balance.

One bar means weak privacy under our tracing model. Even if every simulated trial is traced, that is an attack result, not the same as establishing mathematically necessary funding. This distinction is why simulated results use one to five bars; it is not a cosmetic preference against showing zero. Bars are rounded categories, not exact probabilities. Results with fewer than ten completed trials are not offered.

Why suggest keeping some shielded?

Keeping a remainder changes the public amount pattern. We test the actual deposit and withdrawal together; a good score for sending 30 does not automatically apply to depositing 30 and withdrawing 28.

DepositWithdrawal budgetStays shielded

The recipient receives the withdrawal budget minus fees. We compare up to three common deposit amounts above the budget, capped at 1.5 times it, and test each separately for every allowed wait. At the same privacy level, the smaller remainder wins. An offer appears when it improves the ordinary suggestion's privacy level and your settings allow it; accepting it is optional.

Your maximum wait

Auto allows up to 24 hours. In Custom, a 6 h maximum means only Now and In 6 h are simulated and offered, including suggestions to keep a remainder shielded. Longer trial loops do not run for that request. A 12-hour limit also allows 12 h; None allows only Now. In the model, Now means withdrawing about one minute after depositing.

Where the measurement comes from

The page first uses daily measurements for broad amount ranges. After typing pauses, exact-amount simulations replace them for Privacy Cash and Cloak. If simulation is unavailable, the page retains the broader measurement. Umbra v2 still uses amount-bucket measurements; its shared tree is not supported by the exact-amount simulator yet.

Assumptions and limits

From shielded assumes an older, larger balance: about ten times the payment, deposited three days earlier. The app checks that your balance can fund the payment, but the score does not measure your actual notes' ages or origins. Freshly shielding funds does not automatically earn that score.

The fast probe approximates a full forensic replay. Tests compare their answers, but the probe omits some effects on intervening withdrawals and links from later withdrawals. Split-payment scenarios model two or three recipients rather than your exact allocation. Synthetic recipients also do not carry your real wallet relationships.

The score measures resistance to our current attacks on historical public data. More trials do not account for unknown attacks, future activity, or information held by relayers and exchanges. Escaping these attacks is not proof of anonymity.

Payment links

A payment link is an invoice whose funds land in a privacy pool instead of your wallet. Your browser prepares the private note the payment will fund; DarkSol only stores the invoice and tracks whether it was paid. The payer's wallet deposits straight into the pool, and only your wallet can spend what arrives.

  1. CreateChoose a pool, token and amount, plus an optional reference. Your wallet signs two messages, which are used to create your private merchant ID and the private note your payment is sent to. The signatures stay in your browser and never move funds. Your browser builds the note and sends DarkSol only the public invoice: amount, pool, token and the note's commitment. For Privacy Cash, it also sends the note encrypted under your key.
  2. ShareEach invoice gets its own link, darksol.app/pay/<id>. It does not contain or reveal your wallet address.
  3. PayThe payer connects any Solana wallet and approves one deposit into the pool, funding your note. Right before signing, the checkout reloads the invoice and stops if its terms changed or it was already paid. The transaction goes directly to a Solana RPC, not through DarkSol.
  4. ConfirmThe checkout reports the deposit's public transaction ID. DarkSol checks that the deposit is finalized on-chain and matches the invoice, then marks it paid. If the page closed early, the payer can paste the transaction ID to verify it later.
  5. CollectOpen Collect, enter any destination address and sign the same message again. Your browser proves ownership of each paid note, and the pool's relayer submits the withdrawal and pays its network fee. Two payments in the same pool and token are combined into one withdrawal.

Supported pools and tokens

Payment links work with Privacy Cash and Cloak for SOL, USDC and USDT on Solana mainnet. Cloak requires at least 0.01 SOL or 1 USDC/USDT per payment. The payer pays network fees in SOL; Cloak token deposits also need about 0.006 SOL for a one-time lookup table.

Recovery

Your payment links are tied to your wallet, not to this browser. Signing in with the same wallet on any device finds your links and lets you collect them. The download button in Your payment links saves a backup of every invoice record, so a copy exists outside DarkSol. Deleting a link disables it and removes its record from DarkSol. If the link may already have been paid, choose Download and delete.

Red team

The red team tests privacy from an outside observer's perspective. Using only public Solana data, it takes a withdrawal from a privacy pool and does what a chain analyst would: work out which depositor wallet most likely paid for it, and how many others could have. You can run it yourself on the Trace page in the app.

  1. IndexDarkSol records every deposit, withdrawal and note in the Privacy Cash and Cloak pools for SOL, USDC and USDT.
  2. Replay and verifyEach pool is rebuilt from its first note and checked against the proofs on-chain and the live pool account, so the history a trace relies on matches the chain.
  3. TracePaste a withdrawal's tx hash or Solscan link. The trace lists every note that could have paid for it, then applies the rules below to the wallets that deposited them and sorts those wallets into candidates. Paste a deposit instead to see the withdrawals its wallet is the candidate for. A withdrawal from the last few minutes appears after the next indexing cycle.

Reading a report

Verdict
One strongest candidate when a rule below singles out one depositor; N probable candidates when no rule does, but some wallets have a clue that fits, such as a deposit of the same amount within a minute before; Hidden among N wallets when nothing points to any one of the wallets that could have paid; No single wallet could have paid for it when no depositor had enough left in the pool.
Public clues
Shown first: the deposit of the same amount right before the withdrawal, whether anything else happened in that minute and whether the proof was made right after it, then how many deposits match the amount, how busy the pool was in the minute before, and how common the amount is. Amber marks what makes a withdrawal easier to trace. None of these is proof, and they are never combined into a score.
Candidates
The wallets that could have paid, in three tiers. Strongest: the one wallet a rule names. Probable: wallets with a clue that fits, such as a same-amount deposit, exactly the amount left in the pool, or a deposit right before the proof. When more than six wallets share a clue it no longer narrows anything down, so only the one timing backs stays probable. Possible: the other wallets that had enough in the pool, the most recent first, at most 30.
How this was checked
Collapsed under the report: how the pool history was verified, and what the report cannot tell, such as the hidden amount inside each note, or where money held in the pool first came from. While evidence is still being collected, the figures are bounds, not exact values.

The send-page privacy meter measures synthetic sends against these forensic rules and a timing analyst. It summarizes trial outcomes; the Trace report above analyses an actual transaction.

Research behind the rules

The linking rules build on published research into tracing mixers and shielded pools. The same patterns keep giving users away: the same wallet on both sides, an amount that matches a deposit, and a withdrawal made right after one. We adapted those ideas to Solana's pools.

  • Blockchain is Watching You: Profiling and Deanonymizing Ethereum UsersBéres, Seres, Benczúr and Quintyne-Collins, 2020. Profiles users by address reuse, time of day and fees, and links Tornado Cash deposits to withdrawals.
  • Tutela: An Open-Source Tool for Assessing User-Privacy on Ethereum and Tornado CashWu and others, 2022. Five heuristics that expose Tornado Cash users, and each pool's real anonymity set once those transactions are excluded.
  • On How Zero-Knowledge Proof Blockchain Mixers Improve, and Worsen User PrivacyWang, Chaliasos, Qin, Zhou, Gao, Berrang, Livshits and Gervais, 2023. Shows that advertised anonymity set sizes overstate privacy, with five heuristics that link withdrawers to depositors.
  • On the linkability of Zcash transactionsQuesnelle, 2017. Finds round trips through Zcash's shielded pool, where coins go in and come back out.
  • An Empirical Analysis of Anonymity in ZcashKappos, Yousaf, Maller and Meiklejohn, 2018. Shows that usage patterns, not broken cryptography, make most activity around Zcash's shielded pool attributable.

What a trace cannot tell

Amounts inside notes are hidden, so possible sources can't be filtered by size, and the count is of notes, not people. Notes that were already spent can't be identified, so they stay among the possible sources. Only the last hop is analysed: where money held in the pool first came from is not followed, and the recipient's other activity is not checked yet.

A suspected link is an inference, not proof of ownership. Finding no link does not prove anonymity.

Core concepts

Privacy pool
A protocol that holds shielded funds and verifies private transfers.
Shielding
Moving public funds into a pool. Withdrawing makes the destination and amount public again.
Private note
The secret data used to spend shielded funds. Keep recovery material private.
Privacy estimate
The send-page meter reports how often hypothetical sends escape our tracing model against recorded history. Other planner estimates use pool activity and available cover. Neither is a guarantee; see how the meter is measured.

Architecture

DarkSol connects three parts:

  1. Browser & walletChoose routes, prepare private proofs, and approve transactions.
  2. Privacy protocolsHold shielded funds and verify deposits and withdrawals on-chain.
  3. Data & planningIndexers collect public activity. Stored data powers pool comparisons, privacy estimates, and red-team analysis.

The payment-link service stores invoices and payment status. Payment-note spending keys are derived in your browser from your wallet signature. See Payment links for the full flow.
